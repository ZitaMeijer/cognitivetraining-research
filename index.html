A Ghent University study for the 5th class (ages 10–11)

Can cognitive training support children's learning?

Every participating class practises with a computer-based brain training. We are investigating whether this training helps improve children's reasoning, language and maths skills.

Information for schools Information for parents
Why this research?

The existing evidence is promising, but not conclusive

Scientific research suggests that certain forms of thinking training have the potential to improve children's cognitive and academic skills. At the same time, other studies show that these results are not always consistent.

That is why this study carefully examines how much effect cognitive training has, and whether any effects are maintained over the longer term — using two comparable training versions and measurements at several points in time. Participating schools make a direct contribution to knowledge about effective learning support.

The training

Thinking exercises that systematically get harder

Every participant does thinking exercises on the computer, organised into levels that gradually become more difficult. Through random allocation, each child is assigned to one of two versions of these exercises. Both versions take up an equal amount of time and are aimed at improving children's thinking skills.
