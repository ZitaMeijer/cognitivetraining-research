Information for parents

Your child is invited to take part in a study on brain training

Your child's class is taking part in a Ghent University study on the effect of thinking training on reasoning, language and maths. Below you can read what this means for your child.

What will my child do? FAQ
The training

Thinking exercises that systematically get harder

Every participant does thinking exercises on the computer, organised into levels that gradually become more difficult. Through random allocation, each child is assigned to one of two versions of these exercises. Both versions take up an equal amount of time and are aimed at improving children's thinking skills.

What will my child do?

Your child will practise, together with the class, using a playful computer programme that gradually becomes more difficult. Some sessions take place at school, and part can also be done at home.

Consent and voluntary participation

Participation is entirely voluntary and takes place with your consent as a parent, given by signing the informed consent form. You can withdraw your consent at any time. The study has been approved by the university's ethics committee and complies with GDPR guidelines. Before the start, we organise an information session for parents, teachers and children.

The test sessions

Around the training period, we administer a number of academic and cognitive tests with your child (reading, maths, reasoning), including the Drumcondra tests. This happens partly as a class and partly individually with the research team, at school. These tests are entirely free of charge.

What you will be told

Afterwards, the school receives a report with the results, including your child's individual Drumcondra results. These scores help the school follow up with and support your child in maths and reading. We cannot promise guaranteed effects — the study is precisely meant to find out whether these trainings can help with thinking and academic skills.

Questions

Frequently asked questions from parents

Is participation mandatory?

No. Participation is entirely voluntary and only takes place with your consent as a parent, given by signing the informed consent form. You can withdraw your consent at any time.

Why doesn't every child get the same exercises?

Through random allocation, your child gets one of two versions of the thinking exercises so that we can compare them. Both versions are equally valid — your child does a meaningful activity either way.

Does this cost anything?

No, participation is entirely free. The research team provides all test materials.

What happens to my child's data?

Your child's data is treated confidentially and processed in accordance with GDPR guidelines. The data will be anonymised, so it can no longer be directly linked to your child. There is one exception: the school does receive your own child's individual Drumcondra results, in the same way as with a regular school assessment, so the teacher can follow up with your child. These individual results are not shared with other parties and are not part of the research reporting. Results are only reported at group level, so individual children cannot be identified. The study has been approved by the university's ethics committee.

What if I don't want my child to take part in the study or the training?

That's not a problem. The teacher will provide an alternative activity for your child.

Still have questions?

Before the start, we always organise an information session for parents, teachers and pupils. If you already have a question, please don't hesitate to contact the research team.
