Information for schools

Your 5th class could contribute to research on thinking training

We are investigating whether computer-based brain training can help improve pupils' reasoning, language and maths skills — with free test materials and a report for your school.

Practical details Frequently asked questions
The training

Thinking exercises that systematically get harder

Every participant does thinking exercises on the computer, organised into levels that gradually become more difficult. Through random allocation, each child is assigned to one of two versions of these exercises. Both versions take up an equal amount of time and are aimed at improving children's thinking skills.

Benefits for participating schools

What will your school receive in return?

The school receives a summary report containing the results of all tests at group level, as well as the Drumcondra results for individual pupils. These results can be used to monitor pupils’ progress in vocabulary, reading comprehension and numeracy. Does the school already have a Drumcondra licence? If so, we will reimburse the portion of the licence fee relating to the tests used by the participating class.

Flexible for Your School

We understand that instructional time is valuable and that every school has its own way of working. That is why we work together with schools to ensure that participation fits as smoothly as possible within their daily routines. Below are a few examples of possible adaptations, but we are also open to other suggestions and are happy to explore solutions together.


● 'Light' Training Option
Training sessions can be combined (e.g., 2 × 45 minutes instead of 3 × 30 minutes per week) or partially completed at home. If necessary, sessions can be skipped or the training period can be shortened. Regular lessons and school activities will always remain the priority.

● Integration into Existing Timetables
The training can, for example, be scheduled during ICT/computer lessons, independent work periods, study sessions, or other times when pupils are working independently.

● Support from the Research Team
We provide clear instructions, guidance materials, and support for any practical questions. We actively work with schools to develop a feasible implementation plan. Where possible, we can also provide on-site support during the start-up phase or throughout the training period.

● Flexible Assessment Scheduling
Assessment sessions are scheduled in consultation with the school to minimise disruption to lessons. If necessary, individually administered assessments can also be completed in a shortened format.

Not sure whether participation would be feasible within your school? Please feel free to get in touch. We would be happy to discuss how the study can be adapted to your school's specific needs and circumstances.

Practical details

What more to expect?

The set-up

Pupils are randomly assigned to one of two versions of a computer-based thinking training. The training ideally takes place as a whole class during school hours, supervised by the teacher; part of it can also be done individually at home.

The test sessions

Before and after the training period, we administer a battery of academic and cognitive assessments: reading and maths via the Drumcondra tests, together with additional measures of reasoning (RAI) and general cognitive skills (WISC-V). Some tests are done as a class on the computer, others individually by the research team — we will arrange a suitable time together for this.

What we ask of the teacher

  • scheduling the training sessions within the timetable
  • setting aside time in the timetable for the test sessions
  • supervision during the sessions
  • flagging any practical issues
  • supporting pupils where needed, with guidance from the research team
  • providing an alternative activity for pupils who do not take part in the study
Questions

Frequently asked questions from schools

Does participation cost the school anything?

No. The research team provides all test materials free of charge.

How much time does this ask of the teacher?

The training sessions (e.g. 3 × 30 min/week) are scheduled within the timetable (or given as homework), and supervision is needed during sessions along with time for the test sessions. The frequency is adaptable, e.g. two 45-minute sessions also work. Taking part asks for a commitment from teachers, parents and children. You are not on your own, though: the research team is always reachable and happy to think along about how to organise the study in a practical, workable way within the school.

What if we'd like to take part but aren't sure it fits the timetable?

We're happy to think along about what's feasible for your school and adapt where we can. Is your school interested but unsure whether participation is practically workable? Let us know, and we'll look at the options together.

Why don't all pupils get the same exercises?

Through random allocation, each pupil gets one of two versions of the thinking exercises so that we can compare them. Both versions are equally valid and take the same amount of time — so no pupil is left doing "nothing".

What happens to the data?

Your pupils' data is treated confidentially and processed in accordance with GDPR guidelines. The data will be anonymised, so it can no longer be directly linked to individual pupils. There is one exception: the school does receive the individual Drumcondra results per pupil (see "What the school receives" above), in the same way as with a regular school assessment. The study results are only reported at group level, so individual children cannot be identified. The study has been approved by the university's ethics committee and complies with GDPR guidelines.

Can the training also be done at home?

The training ideally takes place as a whole class during school hours, but part of the sessions can also be done individually at home.

Interested, or have questions?

We're happy to think along about what's feasible for your school. If you're interested, we can arrange a no-obligation (online) introductory conversation.
